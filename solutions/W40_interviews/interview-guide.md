## Target group (proposal — agree with your group)
**We are building for students and young professionals (20–35) who use AI tools every week but haven't made up their minds about it —
they want to understand where they stand compared to others.**
Why: they're easy to reach this week, they match the dataset's 18–34 group (most exposed, slightly more excited), and they are the
typical audience of a web piece or exhibition. Secondary group to consider later: people 50+ who barely use AI (the most concerned group).

## Recruiting (3 people, outside the course, by 8 Oct)
- 1 × student from another department (HSLU Wirtschaft / Design) — campus café
- 1 × working person 25–35 (job, not IT) — friend of a friend, colleague at a part-time job
- 1 × someone who rarely uses AI (contrast case) — family member / neighbour
Don't interview other Informatik students — they think like you.

## Introduction (read out)
"Hi, I'm Adhurim, I study at HSLU. We're working on a project about how people experience artificial intelligence in everyday life.
There are no right or wrong answers — we're interested in your experiences, not your opinion on technology. It takes about 20 minutes.
Is it OK if my colleague takes notes?"

## Broad openers (story questions)
1. Tell me about the last time you came across AI in your everyday life — where were you, what happened?
2. What do you use AI for in a normal week? Walk me through a typical situation.
3. Tell me about a moment when AI surprised you — positively or negatively.

## Deeper questions
4. What happened the last time you weren't sure whether something was real or made by AI? *(links to the 82 % "real vs fake" finding)*
5. How has AI changed anything in your work or studies so far? What did that feel like?
6. When you hear news about AI — what was the last story that stuck with you? What did you do or say afterwards?
7. How do you talk about AI with friends or family? Tell me about the last conversation.
8. What happened the last time you decided *not* to use AI for something?

## Success / criteria questions
9. Imagine you saw a website or exhibit about how people feel about AI. How would you notice afterwards that it was worth your time?
10. Where do you go when you want to find out what "people in general" think about a topic? What happened the last time?

## Follow-ups (use anytime)
"Can you tell me more?" · "What happened next?" · "How did that feel?" · "Why was that important to you?" · (silence)
