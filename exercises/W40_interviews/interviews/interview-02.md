# Interview 02 — Working professional, 29, marketing assistant

- **Who:** P2, marketing assistant, 29. Uses AI at work but is concerned about reliability, privacy and the effect on jobs.
- **Why this person:** primary target group (R6), the work side: AI on the job, time pressure.
- **Date:** October 2026 · **Interviewer:** Adhurim Thaqi (solo project, notes written up after the interview)

## Answers (as given)

**Broad openers**
1. *Last time you came across AI?* — "This morning, I used an AI feature to help me draft a social media caption for work. I gave it some information about a campaign, and it generated a few different versions. I picked one and edited it before using it."
2. *What do you use AI for in a normal week?* — "I use it to brainstorm campaign ideas, improve written communication, summarize information, and sometimes translate short texts. It is mostly a productivity tool for me. I don't use it for everything because I need to make sure the final result fits our company."
3. *A moment when AI surprised you?* — "I once gave it a very short description of a campaign, and it generated several creative ideas that I hadn't considered. I was surprised by how quickly it did that. However, some suggestions were unrealistic, so I still needed to use my own judgment."

**Deeper**

4. *Last time you weren't sure if something was real or AI?* — "I saw an image online that looked like a real photograph of a public figure. The lighting and details looked convincing, but I couldn't find the same image on a reliable news website. I searched for more information before believing it."
5. *How has AI changed your work?* — "It has made some repetitive tasks faster, especially writing first drafts and brainstorming. That gives me more time for other work. But I also feel pressure to produce things faster because people expect AI to make everything easier."
6. *Last AI news story that stuck with you?* — "I read an article about companies using AI to automate jobs. It made me wonder how many tasks in my own role could eventually be automated. I discussed it with a colleague, and we agreed that human creativity and understanding clients are still important."
7. *How do you talk about AI with friends or family?* — "At work, we talk about productivity and whether AI-generated content meets our quality standards. With friends, we talk more about fake images, privacy, and jobs. Everyone seems to have a different level of trust in it."
8. *Last time you decided not to use AI?* — "I was preparing a message for an unhappy client. I decided to write it myself because the situation needed empathy and a personal understanding of the relationship. I didn't want the message to sound generic or artificial."

**Success**

9. *How would you notice a website/exhibit about AI was worth your time?* — "I would want to see credible data, different perspectives, and practical examples. It would be interesting to compare my own views with those of people in different professions. I wouldn't want something that only promotes AI or only focuses on its risks."
10. *Where do you go to find out what people in general think?* — "I usually read news websites and LinkedIn discussions. If I want more personal opinions, I look at Reddit. The challenge is knowing whether the people commenting represent the wider population or just a very vocal group."
