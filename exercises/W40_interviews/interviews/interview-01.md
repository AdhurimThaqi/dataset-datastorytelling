# Interview 01 — Student, 22, business student

- **Who:** P1, business student, 22. Uses AI several times a week for studying, writing and understanding difficult topics.
- **Why this person:** primary target group (R6): weekly AI user, no technical background.
- **Date:** October 2026 · **Interviewer:** Adhurim Thaqi (solo project, notes written up after the interview)

## Answers (as given)

**Broad openers**
1. *Last time you came across AI?* — "Yesterday, I used ChatGPT to explain a topic from one of my lectures. I had read the slides but didn't really understand them, so I asked it to explain everything in simpler words and give me an example."
2. *What do you use AI for in a normal week?* — "I mainly use ChatGPT for studying, summarizing long texts, improving emails, and brainstorming ideas for assignments. Usually, I start with Google, but when I need a clear explanation, I use AI."
3. *A moment when AI surprised you?* — "I was surprised when it explained a difficult topic in a way that made it immediately understandable. But another time, it gave me an answer that sounded completely correct but contained false information. Since then, I check important facts myself."

**Deeper**

4. *Last time you weren't sure if something was real or AI?* — "I saw a video on Instagram that looked realistic, but something about the person's face and movements seemed strange. I checked the comments, and some people said it was AI-generated. I wasn't completely sure, so I didn't share it."
5. *How has AI changed your studies?* — "It saves me time because I can ask questions whenever I get stuck. I feel less dependent on other people for explanations. At the same time, I sometimes worry that I'm learning less if I let AI do too much of the work."
6. *Last AI news story that stuck with you?* — "I remember reading about AI taking over certain jobs. I talked about it with a friend because we were wondering what the job market would look like after graduation. It made me think that we need to learn how to work with AI rather than ignore it."
7. *How do you talk about AI with friends or family?* — "My friends and I often share funny answers or useful prompts. We also discuss whether using AI for assignments counts as cheating. Some people think it's just another tool, while others think it gives students an unfair advantage."
8. *Last time you decided not to use AI?* — "I had to write a personal reflection for a course. I decided to write it myself because I wanted it to sound like me. I only used a spellchecker afterwards."

**Success**

9. *How would you notice a website/exhibit about AI was worth your time?* — "If it showed what other people my age think about AI and included real examples, I would find it interesting. I would also like to see statistics about how people actually use AI, not just whether they like or dislike it."
10. *Where do you go to find out what people in general think?* — "Usually Reddit, YouTube comments, or Instagram polls. Recently, I looked through Reddit discussions about AI in education to see whether other students had the same concerns as me."

## Key quotes → what it means for us
| Quote | Insight |
|---|---|
| Q4: "I wasn't completely sure, so I didn't share it." | **S1:** real vs. fake is a lived, everyday experience. |
| Q5: "It saves me time … At the same time, I sometimes worry that I'm learning less" | **S6:** mixed feelings: benefit and worry at once. |
| Q6: "we need to learn how to work with AI rather than ignore it" | **S2:** acceptance that AI is coming; adapting rather than resisting. |
| Q8: "I wanted it to sound like me." | **S3:** frequent users draw their own lines about where *not* to use AI. |
| Q9: "If it showed what other people my age think about AI" | **S5 → criterion 1:** comparing with peers is what makes it worth the time. |
| Q10: "Reddit, YouTube comments, or Instagram polls" | **S4:** opinions come from social media, not from surveys. |

**Surprise:** P1 wants to know how people *use* AI, not only whether they like it. Our dataset measures feelings, not use → we must say clearly what the data does and doesn't show.
