# Three sentences before we sketch (W42 §6)

Taken from `../W41_concept/concept.md`, `../W41_concept/user-survey.md` and `../W40_interviews/`. No new claims.
Numbers: latest NORC wave, 10–14 Sep 2026, n = 1,083 (run `python data_cut.py`).

**The data shows …**
about 2 in 3 Americans are concerned about AI (68.8 %, `ai_sentiment`), and about 2 in 3 say
"AI is coming into our lives whether we want it or not" (67.5 %, `ai_agency`). Only 17.4 % chose
"people like me can shape how much AI is part of our lives". Among 18–34 it is 26.7 %, among 65+ 8.1 %.

**People told us …**
"Nobody asks us if we want that." (interview 03, 50+, about AI in the news)

**Our question: How might we …?**
How might we let young adults find out whether their feeling about AI is "normal", and show them
that being worried and thinking "it's coming anyway" are both majority views?

- Check from the script: read the three lines to someone from another group. If they can't retell the question, sharpen it. *(to do in class)*
- Careful with the wording: the data has **no crosstab** between the two questions. We can say "2 in 3 are worried *and* 2 in 3 say it's coming",
  not "the worried are the ones who say it's coming". The minimum overlap is 36.3 % of all adults, which is 52.8 % of the concerned.
  That technically supports "most of the worried", but only as a lower bound. → Keep the sentence about two separate majorities.
