# Concept refinement: what the W43 test showed

**Tested:** prototype "Real or AI?" (`../W43_prototyping/prototype/`), v1 → v3.
**Who:** the lecturer (Test 0, v1, 22.10), my own review (v2), three testers (v3, week of 22.10).
**Details:** `../W43_prototyping/prototype/test.md`

## 1 · What we learned
| Finding | Source | Means for the concept |
|---|---|---|
| The **time pressure** is what people like | Lecturer (v1) + all three testers (v3) | Keep it. The clock is now part of the hook, not an extra. |
| It feels like **fun**, and **everything was clear** | All three testers | The hook works without explanation (acceptance 1 ✅). No tutorial needed. |
| It gets **predictable after several plays**: always the same four pictures | All three testers | The guess only works the first time. Replaying turns it into remembering. |
| The end screen was too cramped | My review (v2) | Fixed in v3: result → "Show me →" → the number. |

**Not answered yet (test again in W48):**
- Do people remember the **57** after two minutes? (not recorded this time)
- Did it feel like a **quiz**, or like it was **about them**? That's the hypothesis on the prototype card, and it's still open.

## 2 · What changes (concept v1 → v2)
1. **A bigger picture pool, drawn at random.**
   - at least 12 pictures (6 real, 6 AI)
   - every round draws 4: always 2 real + 2 AI, in random order
   - the same visitor doesn't get a picture twice until the pool is used up
   - **Why:** fixes the predictability. A second play is a new round, not a memory test.
2. **The hook stays short: 4 pictures × 5 seconds.**
   - The bigger pool changes *which* pictures, not *how many*. The whole experience still has to fit in about 2 minutes.
3. **The hook leads into the story.**
   - v3 ends on the 57. The full version goes on to steps 3–6 (your answer → you vs. America → the turn → the close).
   - This also tests the open hypothesis: does the guess make the number feel personal?
4. **"Play again" is offered only at the very end**, after the key message, so replaying doesn't skip the data.

## 3 · What stays the same
- The key message: *Most Americans are worried about AI, and most think it's coming anyway. Younger adults are more likely than older adults to think it can still be shaped.*
- The 6 steps of direction A (`../../README.md`, "Direction A")
- The data rules: latest NORC wave only, pilot excluded, "not sure / no answer" visible, no gap under the margin of error
- Neutral towards AI. The hook asks you to *look*, it doesn't say "be more critical".
